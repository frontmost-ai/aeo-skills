# The list entry

An editor who says yes usually asks for the text. Write an entry that they can paste with few changes.

## Read the neighbors first

Read the target article and three or four of its entries. Copy their form:

- The same section names, in the same order. One list uses Strengths, Downsides, and Pricing. A different list uses Key Features and Best For.
- The same length, to within a few lines.
- The same point of view. Most lists write about each product in the third person.
- The same level of detail on price.

Never paste one entry into two articles. Keep the meaning and change the form for each article.

## What the entry says

1. **First sentence: what the buyer gets.** Say what the product helps the reader do, and for whom. Do not open with a feature list or a price.
2. **Then the proof.** Two or three specific capabilities that support the first sentence. Each one is a fact that the reader can check on the product's site.
3. **A real limitation**, when the article has a section for downsides. Use a true one, such as a plan limit. Do not invent one to fill the heading.
4. **Price**, in the article's format, read from the live pricing page on the day that you write.

## Rules

- Keep the product's real audience. An article for small businesses sets the context. It does not make the product a small-business product.
- Lead with the benefit, and let features support it. A feature that needs an explanation is not the first sentence.
- Read the live site before you write a price, a plan limit, or a feature. An older approved entry does not keep a fact current.
- Do not claim a result, a ranking, or a customer number that the person did not confirm.
- Show the person the entry and the destination article together. Approval of an entry for one article does not cover a changed entry for another article.

## Example

The article's other entries have an opening paragraph, then Strengths, Downsides, and Pricing.

Weak:

> Acme Invoices is a small-business invoicing platform with recurring billing, templates, reminders, multi-currency support, and an API. Plans start at 1%.

It opens with a feature list, narrows the audience to match the article's title, and has no limitation.

Better:

> **Acme Invoices** helps freelancers and small teams get paid on time without a monthly software bill.
>
> **Strengths.** Recurring invoices go out by themselves, and late invoices get a reminder. Clients can pay by card or bank transfer from the invoice.
>
> **Downsides.** There is no time tracking. Teams that bill by the hour need a second tool.
>
> **Pricing.** 1% of each paid invoice. No monthly fee.
